# On Product Judgment

> You don't become a good ~~Communist~~ *Product Manager* by going to meetings or memorizing the [Manifesto](http://agilemanifesto.org). You do it on the streets. You do it with your fists. The rest is bullshit and you know it.
>
> [Gregor Anghel](https://youtu.be/60P5qYQEuIw?t=43)

I love [Comrade Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comrade_Detective), and there's much truth in Detective Anghel's quote. In my experience, good product managers bounced around career-wise before finding their way to the job. Everything prior felt almost-right, and that restlessness resulted in a wide array of experiences they could bring to bear, becoming whatever the moment needed to get the job done. But the truly best product managers I've worked with went on to couple that praxis with theory.

This isn't a substitute for the experience. You'll get that in time.

It's not a list of tools or frameworks. You can find those anywhere.

Instead, it's a set of lessons intended to strengthen your *product judgment*, supplementing what you'll learn on the job with the principles behind the work. It helps you develop an internal compass to navigate situations for which you have no map.

## How it works

- **Twelve modules, each built on one question.** A module takes up to 90 minutes: up to an hour of reading, watching or playing, and about half an hour applying it to your own work.
- **Every module has a short path that's free to read.** Books are on the full path, which is optional. Take it when a question keeps coming up in your work.
- **Every source says why to trust it** and what you'll get from it, so you can decide what's worth your time.
- **Each idea worth keeping gets a concept card, one page long.** Keep them near your work.
- **On the site, your progress stays in your browser.** Nothing is sent anywhere.

## The modules

- **0 Own Your Role.** How do you grow your role beyond what you were handed, and make the product's concept your own? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **1 See the System.** What's the system here, and why does it behave this way? *Ready.*
- **2 Find the Constraint.** Where is the work actually stuck, and are we measuring the right thing? *Ready.*
- **3 Mind the Seams.** How does the way we're organized shape what we build? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **4 Map the Domain.** What questions must the system answer, how fast and how fresh, and where should its boundaries fall? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **5 Design for Change.** What will change, how fast, and what should be easy to change? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **6 Change Safely.** How good does it have to be, and how do we change it quickly when mistakes are expensive? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **7 Weigh the Evidence.** What do the numbers and the stories each tell you, and how much should either decide? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **8 Decide and Set Direction.** Which decisions matter, whose are they, and what are we choosing not to do? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **9 Answer for What You Build.** Who benefits and who is harmed by what we build, and what is our share of the responsibility? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **10 Notice When You Are Wrong.** How do we find out we are wrong? *Not rebuilt yet.*
- **11 Bring People With You.** How do you earn the trust of the people who decide and depend on you, and keep it when there is nothing to report? *Not rebuilt yet.*
