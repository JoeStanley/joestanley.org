The dice game

{{- partial "pm/margin.html" (dict "p" .p "unit" .unit "slot" "dice-game") }}

Five people pass work down a line, left to right. Each round, everyone rolls a die in turn and passes on that many items, or everything they have if that is fewer. The first person never runs out.

A die averages 3.5, so over 20 rounds the averages promise 70 finished items.

Before you play, guess. How many will the line finish in 20 rounds?

The line finished {{ $d.example.finished }} of the 70 the averages promised. Stations 4 and 5 rolled well above average, and it didn't help: they had nothing extra to pass on. Simulated 400,000 times, this line finishes {{ $first.average }} on average.

What to take away

  1. Equal capacity doesn't deliver the average, because each step can only pass on what has reached it.
  2. The pile shows you where the constraint is.
  3. Speed added anywhere else mostly becomes a queue, not output.

What the game leaves out. Real steps aren't dice. People help each other out, and the constraint moves once you've relieved it. The game shows why a line falls short of its averages. It can't tell you where yours is stuck, and your map of waiting times can.
