> You don't become a good {{< pm/edit "Communist" "Product Manager" >}} by going to meetings or memorizing the [Manifesto](http://agilemanifesto.org). You do it on the streets. You do it with your fists. The rest is bullshit and you know it.
>
> [Gregor Anghel](https://youtu.be/60P5qYQEuIw?t=43)

[Comrade Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comrade_Detective) was an over-the-top delight, but there's some truth in Detective Anghel's words. In my experience, good product managers bounced around career-wise before finding their way to the job. Everything prior felt almost-right, and that circuitous journey resulted in a broad set of experiences they could bring to bear, becoming whatever the moment needed to get the job done. But the truly best product managers I've worked with went on to couple that praxis with theory, which is the goal of this curriculum.

This isn't a substitute for the experience. You'll get that in time.

It's not a list of tools or frameworks. You can find those anywhere.

Instead, it's a set of lessons intended to strengthen your **{{< pm/term "product-judgment" >}}**, supplementing what you'll learn on the job with the principles behind the work. Put another way, it helps you develop an internal compass to navigate situations for which you have no map, by helping you identify the right questions to get answered before making a call on matters where there's rarely one right answer.

One last call-out: This curriculum is intended for early- to mid-career product managers, and draws its examples from a mix of industries. The more senior you are within that spectrum, the more you've likely learned these lessons in part. If you're a senior product leader, I hope this material resonates enough that you share it with the target audience within your org.

## How it works

- **Thirteen modules, each built on one question, then a close.** A module takes up to 90 minutes: roughly an hour of reading, watching or playing, and the rest applying it to your own work. Reading a module's own page, with its log line and Check, takes about 20 minutes more. At about a module a week, the whole course takes a quarter.

- **From {{< pm/mod "know-who-its-for" >}} and on, you'll keep a decision log:** one call a module, what you expect to happen, how sure you are as a percentage, and when you'll know. Intuition earns trust where a situation has [patterns you can learn](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016755), which requires a feedback loop. However, product calls can take months to play out, so where you can, log one you'll know about within the quarter. The closing page, [The Compass](/pm/the-compass/), has you look back over it. It may feel silly, but this is one of the most important parts of the entire curriculum: making it real in a place you can safely fail.

- **Some exercises end with a question for a colleague.** That part sits outside the module's time. Ideally, tell some of your engineering and business colleagues in advance that you're doing these modules, and that you may come to them along the way for feedback on tasks that intersect with their disciplines. Most people welcome a product manager who wants to become a better working partner, and will create capacity in their schedule to help. It also gives you an excuse to deepen professional relationships, if you need one.

- **Every module has a short path that's free to read.** Books are on the full path, which is optional. Take it when a question keeps coming up in your work.

- **Every source says why to trust it,** starting with its kind of evidence: a study, a review of studies, experience or an argument. It says what you'll get from it, too, so you can decide what's worth your time.

  The exception to this is one story per module, usually a movie or a show, which conveys the module's concepts in some way. While fictional or dramatized, a single compelling story is more memorable than a stack of research.

- **Each idea worth keeping gets a concept card, one page long.** Keep them near your work.

- **On the site, your progress stays in your browser.** Nothing is sent anywhere. Tick each step of a short path as you finish it. Its last two are the Do and the Check, so a module reads Done once you've done everything in it but the full path. How well you did them is yours to judge.
