> You don't become a good {{< pm/edit "Communist" "Product Manager" >}} by going to meetings or memorizing the [Manifesto](http://agilemanifesto.org). You do it on the streets. You do it with your fists. The rest is bullshit and you know it.
>
> [Gregor Anghel](https://youtu.be/60P5qYQEuIw?t=43)

I love [Comrade Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comrade_Detective), and there's much truth in Detective Anghel's quote. In my experience, good product managers bounced around career-wise before finding their way to the job. Everything prior felt almost-right, and that restlessness resulted in a wide array of experiences they could bring to bear, becoming whatever the moment needed to get the job done. But the truly best product managers I've worked with went on to couple that praxis with theory.

This isn't a substitute for the experience. You'll get that in time.

It's not a list of tools or frameworks. You can find those anywhere.

Instead, it's a set of lessons intended to strengthen your **product judgment**, supplementing what you'll learn on the job with the principles behind the work. It helps you develop an internal compass to navigate situations for which you have no map. {{< pm/voice "what-judgment-is" >}}{{< pm/voice "why-now" >}}

## How it works

- **Thirteen modules, each built on one question, then a closing page.** A module takes up to 90 minutes: up to an hour of reading, watching or playing, and the rest applying it to your own work. [Answer for What You Build](/pm/answer-for-what-you-build/) runs longer, on purpose. Reading a module's own page, with its log line and Check, takes about 20 minutes more. At about a module a week, the whole course takes a quarter.
- **From [Know Who It's For](/pm/know-who-its-for/) on, you'll keep a decision log:** one call a module, what you expect to happen, how sure you are as a percentage, and when you'll know. Intuition earns trust where a situation has patterns you can learn and you get clear feedback on your calls ([Kahneman and Klein, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016755)). Product calls can take months to play out, so where you can, log one you'll know about within the quarter. The closing page, [The Compass](/pm/the-compass/), has you look back over it, and names [what the course leaves out](/pm/the-compass/#what-the-course-leaves-out).
- **Some exercises end with a question for a colleague.** That part sits outside the module's time. Where the answers can wait, save them up for a month, and ask each person theirs in one conversation.
- **Every module has a short path that's free to read.** Books are on the full path, which is optional. Take it when a question keeps coming up in your work.
- **Every source but a story says why to trust it,** starting with its kind of evidence: a study, a review of studies, experience or an argument. It says what you'll get from it, too, so you can decide what's worth your time.
- **Each idea worth keeping gets a concept card, one page long.** Keep them near your work.
- **On the site, your progress stays in your browser.** Nothing is sent anywhere. Tick each step of a short path as you finish it. Its last two are the Do and the Check, so a module reads Done once you've done everything in it but the full path. How well you did them is yours to judge.
