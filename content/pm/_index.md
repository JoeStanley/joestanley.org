> You don't become a good {{< pm/edit "Communist" "Product Manager" >}} by going to meetings or memorizing the [Manifesto](http://agilemanifesto.org). You do it on the streets. You do it with your fists. The rest is bullshit and you know it.
>
> [Gregor Anghel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comrade_Detective)

In my experience, good product managers bounced around career-wise before finding their way to the job. Everything prior felt almost-right, and that restlessness resulted in a wide array of experiences they could bring to bear, becoming whatever the moment needed to get the job done. But the best product managers went back and coupled that praxis with theory.

This isn't a substitute for the experience. You'll get that in time.

It's not a list of tools or frameworks. You can find those anywhere.

Instead, it's a set of lessons intended to strengthen your *product judgment*, supplementing what you'll learn on the job with the principles behind the work. It helps you develop an internal compass to navigate situations for which you have no map.

## How it works

- **Twelve modules, one question each.** A module takes up to 90 minutes: up to an hour of reading, watching or playing, and about half an hour applying it to your own work.
- **The short path is free to read.** Books are on the full path, which is optional. Take it when a question keeps coming up in your work.
- **Every source says why to trust it** and what you'll get from it, so you can decide what's worth your time.
- **Concept cards are one page each.** Keep them near your work.
- **On the site, your progress stays in your browser.** Nothing is sent anywhere.
