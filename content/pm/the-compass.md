## One call, walked through

Real calls need several questions at once. Here's one, on Own Your Role's music streaming app: should you retire the old version of the app, the one that still runs on older phones?

Start with who it's for. Ask the people still on it about the last time they tried to update, not whether they'd mind (Know Who It's For). Then look at the system. The old version's support tickets pile up like water in a tub (See the System), and if the engineers who answer them are the ones every release waits on (Find the Constraint), retiring it speeds up everything behind it.

Check what the evidence leaves out. The dashboard says few people still use the old version. Weigh the Evidence would ask what the old version reports at all, and who's on it: people who can't afford a new phone, or who were handed down an old one.

Ask what kind of door it is. Switching the old version off can be undone, but what its listeners saved on their phones may not come back, since the data a feature creates outlives the feature (Design for Change). Treat that part as a one-way door (Decide and Set Direction), and give people a way to keep it before anything goes dark.

Here two questions collide. Know Who It's For and Change Safely say waiting is expensive, and small steps you can undo are safe. Answer for What You Build asks who pays, and here it's the people with the oldest phones, who have the least say. Neither wins by rule. Ask whether the cost can be designed away rather than passed on: a longer notice, a web player that runs on old phones, an export for what they saved. If it can, retire the old version on a date, with a guardrail for those listeners, such as how many stop listening altogether (Change Safely). If it can't, say so plainly, and decide with the cost in view.

Last, decide who hears it, and when (Bring People With You): the listeners on the old version first, from the app, with a date, and the support team who'll answer them. Then log the call, with what you expect and the result that would make you reopen it (Notice When You Are Wrong).

## Do

**Review your decision log (20 minutes).** Read back every call you've logged since Know Who It's For. For each one, write what you predicted, how sure you were, and what happened. Mark the ones you can't judge yet, and look again in a month. Where were you sure and wrong? Where were you right for a reason that turned out not to matter? For each miss, find the module whose question would have changed the call, and write that question beside it.

**Walk one call of your own (10 minutes).** Walk a call in front of you now, as the old app was walked. Which questions does it need, and where do two of them collide? Log it, with what you expect.

## Where to go from here

Come back to a module when its question keeps coming up in your work, and take its full path then.

One judgment ran through the course without a name: where to draw the line. See the System drew it around a system, and asked whose costs fall outside it. Mind the Seams drew it between teams and between the categories people are sorted into, Design for Change around what's likely to change, and Rule Zero around your own job. Wherever you draw a line, ask what it leaves out.
