## The idea

Every system has one part that limits what the whole can deliver: its {{< pm/term "constraint" >}}. Eliyahu Goldratt's five steps say what to do about it, in this order:

1. **Identify** the constraint.
2. **Exploit** it: get the most from it as it is.
3. **Subordinate** everything else to that decision.
4. **Elevate** it: invest to raise its capacity.
5. **Repeat** from step 1 once the constraint has moved, and do not let old habits become the new constraint.

## Why a PM cares

- **Effort anywhere else is wasted.** Speeding up a step that is not the constraint only builds a longer queue in front of the one that is.
- **The order saves money.** Steps 2 and 3 cost little. Step 4 costs budget, so it comes last. Asking for more people first skips the cheaper steps.
- **The constraint may be a rule, not a team.** An approval board that meets monthly, or a sign-off only one person can give, limits delivery as surely as a short-staffed team, and is often easier to change.

## How to spot it

- Work piles up in front of one step, while the steps after it wait for something to do.
- Everyone is busy and delivery is still slow.
- Knowledge work hides its queues in inboxes, tickets and approval calendars. Count the {{< pm/term "wip" "work in progress" >}} waiting at each step, using ticket or approval dates.

## What it does not say

It does not say the other steps do not matter. It says their pace should follow the constraint's. It is also a lens, not a proven law: a 2009 literature review found it had yet to show its effect in academic studies.
