## The idea

Ask about the last time it happened, not whether they'd want it. A forecast of your own behavior is a guess, and last Tuesday is evidence. Teresa Torres puts it plainly: "Our past behavior is much more predictive of our future behavior than any prediction we might make ourselves about our future behavior." Then ask what they did about it and what it cost them, which sets a ceiling on what a fix is worth to them.

## Why a PM cares

- **"Would you" builds for the customer people imagine being.** A roadmap built on those answers gets features everyone said yes to and nobody uses.
- **The workaround is the competition.** The spreadsheet, the phone call, the second app: whatever they did last time is what your feature has to beat.
- **The cost is the price ceiling.** What the problem cost them last time, in money or hours, tells you what a fix is worth to them.

## How to spot it

- An interview question starts with "Would you," "Do you ever" or "How important is."
- A research summary says "users want" with no instance behind it.
- A survey asks people to predict what they'll pay.

## What it does not say

It doesn't say people's opinions are worthless. They tell you what someone believes about themselves, which matters for how you describe the product. They're just poor evidence of what someone will do.
